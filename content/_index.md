This is a reference course in **Modern Standard Arabic** —
{{< ar "اللُّغَةُ الْعَرَبِيَّةُ الْفُصْحى" tr="al-luġa al-ʿarabiyya al-fuṣḥā" >}} — written for
learners in the German *Gesamtschule* system and for independent
self-studiers. Everything on this site is free, openly licensed and
downloadable: the unit pages, the slide decks, the worksheets, the exams and
the audio.

The course begins **before A1**. Arabic is written in an alphabet most learners
have never read, from right to left, with the short vowels usually left off the
page. A separate script stage teaches that alphabet properly — twenty-eight
letters in four positional shapes, the sun and moon letters, the hamza, the
tāʾ marbūṭa, and how to read a fully vocalised word aloud — before any grammar
is asked of you. From there the course runs A1 → A2 → B1.

## What this course teaches, and what it does not

Arabic is **diglossic**. The written, formal, pan-Arab standard — Modern
Standard Arabic, or {{< ar "الفُصْحى" tr="al-fuṣḥā" >}} — is the language of
newspapers, broadcasting, literature, official speech and schooling. It is
nobody's mother tongue: at home, in the market and among friends people speak a
regional variety. A course cannot teach both at once without teaching neither,
so this one teaches MSA as its spine.

It does not pretend the other half does not exist. The recurring cast lives in
**Damascus** — {{< ar "دِمَشْق" tr="Dimašq" >}} — and every unit carries a short
*In Damascus you would hear…* note showing the Levantine form beside the
standard one. The note is a signpost, never a second syllabus.

{{< callout type="note" title="Scope, declared honestly" >}}
The course targets the `core` conformance level of the
[boulingua curriculum framework](https://github.com/boulingua/curriculum):
every in-scope CEFR scale that carries an official A1, A2 or B1 descriptor.
**B2 and C1 are declared gaps**, not quietly omitted — openly-licensed Arabic
material rarely reaches them, and Arabic at B2 is a multi-year undertaking that
this course does not claim to deliver.
{{< /callout >}}

## How a unit is built

Every unit is one page and follows the same five moves: **Objectives → Input →
Practise → Produce → Reflect**. Each carries its CEFR can-do statements in the
page's front matter, a slide deck and a worksheet as downloads, and native-voice
audio for the vocabulary and the dialogue. Exams are separate pages, one per
three or four units plus an end-of-level paper, with a printable PDF.

Arabic on this site is real Arabic script, never transliteration standing in for
it. A light DIN 31635 transliteration accompanies **new** vocabulary up to A2 and
is then dropped. Short-vowel marks follow the same fade: fully vocalised in the
script stage and at A1, reduced at A2, absent in B1 running text — which is how
Arabic is actually read.
