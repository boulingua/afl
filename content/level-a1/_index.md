Twelve units for absolute beginners who can already read a vocalised word.
Greetings and introductions, family, the café, numbers to twenty, finding your

## Entry condition

A1 assumes the [script stage]({{< relref "/script" >}}). You should be able to
read a fully vocalised word aloud, recognise every letter in all four of its
positional shapes, and write your own name in Arabic. Nothing else is assumed —
no grammar, no vocabulary.

## The recurring cast

The same handful of people appear across all three levels, so that vocabulary
and story compound instead of resetting every unit.

- **Layla** — {{< ar "لَيْلى" tr="Laylā" >}} — a student, nineteen, born in
  Damascus. The first protagonist.
- **Karīm** — {{< ar "كَريم" tr="Karīm" >}} — runs a small café off a lane in the
  Old City. Most of the course's conversations happen at one of his tables.
- **Samīr and Nadā** — {{< ar "سَمير" tr="Samīr" >}} and
  {{< ar "نَدى" tr="Nadā" >}} — Layla's brother and mother.
- **The Old City** — {{< ar "الْمَدينَةُ الْقَديمَة" tr="al-madīna al-qadīma" >}} —
  the setting: the lanes, the market, the bakery, the bus that never quite comes.

## The grammar spine of A1

A1 Arabic is the nominal sentence and the singular verb, and very little else.
In order:

1. **The nominal sentence** — {{< ar "الْبَيْتُ كَبيرٌ" tr="al-baytu kabīrun" gloss="the house is big" >}}: no copula, the predicate indefinite.
2. **Definiteness** — the article {{< ar "الـ" tr="al-" >}} and its assimilation to the sun letters, met already in the script stage.
3. **Gender** — the tāʾ marbūṭa {{< ar "ـة" >}} and adjective agreement.
4. **The possessive** — attached pronouns: {{< ar "بَيْتي" tr="baytī" gloss="my house" >}}.
5. **The present tense, singular** — three persons, and the negation {{< ar "لا" tr="lā" >}}.
6. **Question words** — {{< ar "مَنْ · ما · أَيْنَ · مَتى · كَمْ" tr="man · mā · ayna · matā · kam" >}}.

Case endings are shown in vocalised text throughout but are not examined at A1
beyond recognising them; they are actively produced from A2.

{{< callout type="note" title="Vocalisation and transliteration at A1" >}}
Every Arabic string on an A1 page is fully vocalised. DIN 31635 transliteration
accompanies **new** vocabulary only — never a repeated word, and never a whole
sentence — so that the transliteration never becomes the thing being read.
{{< /callout >}}

## In Damascus you would hear…

Each unit closes with a short Levantine note: the same exchange as a Damascene
would actually say it. At A1 these are pronunciation and one or two everyday
substitutions — {{< ar "شو؟" tr="šū" gloss="what?" >}} for
{{< ar "ما؟" tr="mā" >}}, for instance. They are marked, brief, and never
examined. The examined language is the standard.
